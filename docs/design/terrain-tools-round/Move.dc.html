Move — select voxels and drag them

The first verb in Sculpt, and a verb of Select › Region, which is where the 2026-09-12 ruling put it. With a selection, drag it: it slides on the plane of the face you grabbed. Or drag one of the three axis handles to keep it on one axis. It moves a whole tile at a time sideways and a half tile up and down, a ghost shows where it will land, and the distance reads out beside the pointer and can be typed.

What it leavesAir. Alt leaves a copy, which is Duplicate. The faces it uncovers follow the New faces switch.
What it lands onIt replaces whatever solid is there, the way a paste does, and the ghost shows the overlap in red before release. A switch offers Merge, which keeps what is there and adds only into air.
Its paintTravels with it, face for face, including pasted tiles. Faces that end up buried lose their stacks, as the no-dormant-paint ruling requires; faces that become exposed by the move are new faces.
What stands on itObjects and structures standing on the selection move with it, as they already re-ground when sculpting moves a surface. Water in it moves; water it displaces drains.
EdgesPer-edge exceptions move with the edges that still exist afterwards.
Off the mapIt stops at the map's bounds and the height ceiling; the ghost shows the clamp.

Rotate and Mirror sit beside Move: quarter turns about the vertical, and a flip, with ramps turning as they should. Nudge with the arrow keys moves a tile, with PgUp / PgDn for height, matching how objects nudge today.

Move, Extrude and Raise are one family

All three drag a selection along an axis. Move takes the voxels with it and leaves air. Extrude leaves them where they are and fills the gap behind the moving face. Raise is Extrude by a fixed strength. One handle, one ghost, one readout, and the verb says which of the three the drag means.
