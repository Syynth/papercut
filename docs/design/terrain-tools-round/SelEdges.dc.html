What an edge is — more than today's two per wall

Built so far: a wall's top and its foot, because that is what a fringe and a picket hang on. For selecting, an edge is any line where the terrain's surface turns, and each knows its kind:

KindWhereWhat stands on it
TopA cliff's upper lip: level ground meets a wall going down.fringe
FootA wall's base: a wall meets the ground it stands on.picket
SlopeA ramp's open side, and its head and foot.rail, landing
CornerUpright: where two walls meet, turning out or in.a wall's seam art

Tops and feet keep the address they have; slope and corner edges, and edges under an overhang, need the fuller one: a face and which of its four sides.

Same kind, same trim — the two Matches that are about what an edge carries

Same kind takes connected edges of the clicked edge's kind whatever way they turn. Same trim takes edges in the same state: on, switched off, or drawing another material's trim. With Everywhere, that is "every fringe I have switched off".

Run, and Loop

click: one edge
double-click, Run: the full straight edge
triple-click, Loop: all the way round

A run is the straight line of edges of one kind at one height: one side of a plateau, one wall's foot. It stops where the line turns or the height changes. A loop carries on round the corners until it comes back to where it started, or runs out: the whole rim of a plateau, the whole foot of a building.

the same loop, at the foot

from the side: Follow slopes carries a loop down a ramp's side

Follow slopes is a switch beside Match, on by default: a rim that reaches a ramp carries on down its open side and picks up the rim below, which is what a handrail and a fringe both want. Off, a loop stays at one height.

Pair

One verb in the bar, Pair: every selected top also takes its wall's foot, and every foot its top. It is the quick way from "this rim" to "this whole cliff's trims".
