Selecting: one way of working, three elements
Proposed. The aim is that it stays simple: three gestures and one menu, and everything specialised is what a double-click means.

Three gestures, the same for every element

GestureWhat it takes
ClickOne edge, face or voxel: the one under the pointer.
DragWhat the footprint covers: a brush, a rectangle, a lasso. As built on 2026-09-20.
Double-clickThe whole it belongs to, by the Match rule: the full edge, the flat it is part of, the island it is part of. This is the one that does the work.
Triple-clickThe next whole out: the edge loop, every connected face that matches whatever the plane, everything connected.

Shift adds, alt takes away, on every one of them, so a loop can be double-clicked and one side alt-dragged back out. Nothing else to learn: the specialised selections on the next three boards are all what double-click means for that element.

See it before you take it

Hover shows what a click would take, in a lighter tint than the selection. Holding ctrl shows what a double-click would take, so a Match rule can be tried by looking, without selecting and undoing. The status bar names it: edge loop · 24 edges.

The default is the one wanted most, so the menu can usually be ignored. Connected / Everywhere says whether a match has to touch what was clicked, or is taken across the whole map within the layer view: Everywhere is "select similar".

The Fill footprint built on 2026-09-20 goes: it was a double-click all along. Grow and Shrink follow the Match rule too, so growing a Flat selection stays on its plane, as it already does.

Changing element converts the selection

Switching Voxel → Face → Edge with something selected carries it across: voxels become their outward faces, faces become the edges round them; and back the other way, edges become the faces they bound, faces the voxels behind them. So "switch off the fringe all round this hill" is: double-click the hill, press Edge, press Off.
