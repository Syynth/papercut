With glyphs this small, Match can be a row of buttons instead of a menu: four or five per element, all visible, each with its name and what it takes in the tooltip. That is simpler than a dropdown, since nothing is hidden and the active rule is always in view.

The rules the set follows

One idea per glyph, said with weight. The app's grid and 1.7 stroke. What the icon is about is heavy (a 3.2 stroke) or solid; its context is the same line at 40 %. So Face, Edge, Wall and Band are all the same cube, and the eye goes to the part that differs.

Three families, three views. Elements and wall rules are the cube. Edge rules are a plan, a rectangle seen from above, because a run and a loop are about going round. Voxel rules are an elevation, seen from the side, because an island and a layer are about height. Face rules on a floor are a plane, the tilted square.

Dashes mean "the gesture", solids mean "the thing". Brush, Rectangle, Lasso and Box are dashed: they are how you sweep. Everything that names what gets selected is solid.
