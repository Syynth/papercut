Select, then act — your direction, 2026-09-20

The selection drives the terrain tools. What is selected is what a verb changes, and how you select is the one Footprint control this round already shares between modes. That turns the earlier "mask" idea the right way round: the selection is not a limit on a brush, it is the subject of the verb.

1 · select
2 · pick a verb: a handle and a ghost
3 · drag or type, and it lands
With a selectionWhat the verb does
MoveDrags the selected voxels along any axis. Its own board.
Raise / ExtrudePulls the selection's faces out along their normal, by the strength or by the drag. A handle sits on each side that can move; drag the one you mean.
FlattenFills and carves the selection to the plane of the face you press, or to a typed height.
Smooth, RampRelax the selection's tops; lay a slope across it between its high and low ends.
Fill, CarveThe selection becomes solid with the picked Piece, or air. With Depth set to Box, this is the Box shape fill: the other shape fills are selections with a shape.
Edges, PaintSwitch the trims on the selection's edges; put the material on its faces.

And with nothing selected — a stroke is a selection that lasts one press

Brushing a hillside up should not take two steps. With nothing selected, a verb's press takes the footprint under the pointer as its selection, acts, and lets it go. It is the same code path and the same ghost; the only difference is how long the selection lives. So there is one model, and quick sculpting keeps its speed.

What this changes in the frame

Select › Region is where a selection is made, as the 2026-09-12 ruling says, and the selection survives into Terrain, where the verbs are. To save the round trip, holding S in Terrain borrows the Select footprint for as long as it is held, and Esc clears the selection before it leaves the tool. The Terrain bar shows the selection as a chip: its size, and a clear button.
