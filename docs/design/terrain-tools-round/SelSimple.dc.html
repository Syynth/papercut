Keeping it simple: what the bar shows

The bar changes with the element, and shows only what that element uses. Everything else lives behind Match, which has a sensible default, so a first-time user sees five controls and can ignore four of them.

Combine (replace, add, subtract, intersect) leaves the bar: shift and alt already do add and subtract, replace is the default, and intersect moves into the Match menu's foot as "within the selection". That is four buttons fewer on every bar.

An order to build in

#PieceWhy there
1Double-click and Match, with the defaults: Run, Flat, Island. Hover preview.The most selection for the least interface. Fill retires into it.
2Loop and triple-click; Follow slopes; Pair.Needs the run-walker from 1.
3The rest of Match: Material, Tile, Surface with Step, Wall and Band; Layer, Column, Same piece; Same kind, Same trim; Everywhere.Each is one rule for the same flood.
4Converting the selection when the element changes.Needs every element's neighbours, which 1–3 give.
5The fuller edge: slope and corner edges, and edges under overhangs.A change to how an edge is addressed; rails are what need it first.
6Lasso; Box depth and air in a voxel region.Box waits on a verb that wants air: Fill or a shape fill.
7Move.Could go first instead: it needs only voxels and a drag. Your call.
Questions for you.
1 · Is double-click the right gesture for "the whole", with triple for the loop, or would you rather a modifier-click, as a modeller's alt-click loop is?
2 · Do the defaults read right: Run for an edge, Flat for a face, Island for a voxel?
3 · Should a selection be undoable, in the same history as edits? A long selection lost to a stray click is the case for it.
4 · Move before or after the rest of this?
