The reframe

A transition between materials is the thing that exists

Today a tile says which TERRAINS it shows, a terrain belongs to one sheet, and the atlas takes an authored tile only when every terrain at the corner lives in the same sheet — so two materials drawn on different sheets can never meet, whatever anybody draws. Turn it round. What the project holds is a transition: the materials that meet, the orientation it is for, and where its block of art sits. Terrain stops being a noun; a material is the only thing the artist names.

Today — two vocabularies, one sheet at a time
// a terrain set, per sheet
image mapkit.png
  terrains  grass, sand, stone
  tiles     38 → [grass grass grass grass]
            … 345 more

// a material points into one of them,
// and carries a second ref for its sides
material Grass  top: mapkit/grass
material Wood   top: walls/wood-top
                side: walls/wood

The sheet is a boundary and the top/side split is a field on the material. A corner is answered by searching one sheet.

Proposed — one vocabulary, art anywhere
// materials, each with where it belongs
material Grass  floor
material Sand   floor
material Wood   wall

// transitions: who meets, facing what, where
transition [Grass, nothing]     floor  at 1024
transition [Grass, Sand]        floor  at 1120
transition [Grass, Wood]        floor  at 5300  ← other sheet
transition [Grass, Wood]        wall   at 5360  ← overload
transition [Grass, Sand, Wood]  floor  at 2400

No sheet boundary, and the same two materials may have one block for the floor and another for the wall.

What it settles

1

Art stops being trapped in its sheet. The atlas refuses an authored tile today unless every terrain at the corner shares one sheet (sheets.size === 1, atlas.ts). A transition names where its own block is, so a kit's grass can meet another sheet's stone.

2

Sparse art stops being a hole in a grid. There is no sheet-shaped layout to fill, so art covering a few combinations is a few transitions rather than a mostly empty sheet.

3

One vocabulary. Material is what a voxel is made of and what a transition is between. Terrain survives only inside a block, as how its tiles are read, and never reaches the artist.

4

Walls and floors meet properly. A material carries the orientation it belongs to, and a transition carries the orientation it is for, so where a wooden wall meets grass is a block somebody drew rather than something the side field has to imply.

5

Four-way corners become authorable. They composite today because nothing holds them. A four-material block is 24 tiles and closes the model.

This replaces today's role and both terrain refs. A material no longer names art at all; the transitions do.

A transition
fieldwhat
materialstwo, three or four, by id. nothing may be one, which makes it an edge set.
orientationfloor wall any — which faces it draws. Two transitions may share materials and differ here.
ata GID: the top-left tile of its block, addressed across the whole project

The block's shape is derived from how many materials there are, never stored. Papercut parses the block at at and every tile in it is known without a tag being written.

Resolving a corner
1

Take the materials at the four positions and the face's orientation.

2

Look for a transition over exactly that set of materials whose orientation matches; then one marked any.

3

Its block says which of its tiles shows this arrangement. Draw it.

4

No transition? Composite from the edge sets in priority order, exactly as now, and count it as still to author.
