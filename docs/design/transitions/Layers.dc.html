Feeling it out · how the pieces sit

Four layers, and the artist only touches two

The trouble with the model as it stood was that every concept was in the artist's face at once: terrains, sheets, tags, materials with a top and a side. Stacked properly, most of it is machinery.

Block

the artist

What you place. A rule that says what each face is made of given what is around it. This is the only thing on the palette.

Material

the artist

Art for a face. It owes the fifteen-tile edge set its archetype's plane asks for, and it may meet other materials.

Grass   archetype: ground
Dirt    archetype: wall
Cobble  archetype: ramp

Archetype

papercut

Which plane a face's four corner cells lie in, how big its tiles are, and which seams it has. NOT a second matching scheme: the sixteen-tile corner set is the same for every archetype.

ground  corners in map x/y     d × d
wall    corners in run/height  d × d   + 2 seams
ramp    corners in run/across  d × round(d√2)

Transition

the artist, rarely

A block of art where two to four materials genuinely meet, in one archetype. Only where somebody draws one; everything else composites.

[Grass, Sand]        ground  at gid 1120
[Grass, Dirt]        wall    at gid 5360
Reading a face
1

The voxel's block rule says what this face is made of. One material, usually.

2

The four cells at each of its corners give the sixteen-tile mask, in whichever plane the archetype names.

3

If every cell holds the same material, take that material's own edge set. This is nearly every face on a map.

4

If they do not, look for a transition over exactly those materials, in that archetype.

5

No transition? Composite from the materials' own edge sets in priority order, and count it as still to author — which is what papercut does now.

Still unsettled, and worth arguing before anything is built
?

Does a block paint materials onto faces ONCE, or does the voxel remember its block and re-derive forever? The second survives sculpting; the first lets an artist override one face and keep it.

?

Can a block rule see a NEIGHBOUR'S block, so "earth beside stone" can be said? That is the difference between a rule and a lookup table.

?

Are a wall's two seams — convex and concave, where faces meet at an angle — art an artist draws, or geometry the mesher miters? The brief's profile strip suggests the second.

?

Does the ramp archetype need a direction, or does the mesher rotate one set of art through four?
