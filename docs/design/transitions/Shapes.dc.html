The corner model already has the corners. Sixteen, not forty-seven.

Inside and outside corners are not something to add to the corner model — they are already in it. One material against nothing is sixteen arrangements, fifteen of them drawn, and those fifteen ARE the vocabulary. Nothing here needs eight-neighbour matching, and authoring stays at sixteen tiles a pairing.

That is the whole edge set, and it is what an artist already draws. A blob set spends 47 tiles saying the same thing because it asks about eight neighbours per cell; the dual grid asks about four cells per corner and gets there in fifteen.

So what ISN'T a corner?

Only two things, and neither is a tile in the set.

1

Which plane the four cells are in. A floor's corner sits between four cells of the map. A wall's corner sits between four cells of the wall — two along its run, two up its height. Same sixteen arrangements, different plane. That is what an archetype settles.

2

The seam where two faces meet at an angle. Where a wall turns a corner in plan, two wall faces meet along a vertical edge. That seam is between faces, not inside one, so no corner tile can describe it. A convex seam and a concave seam are the pieces walls need that floors do not.

Three short rows, not a bag of a dozen shapes. The sixteen-tile set is the same for every archetype; what changes is where its cells are and how big its tiles are.
