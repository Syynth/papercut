Feeling it out · the artist's object

A block is what you place. It decides its own faces.

A material is art for one face. That is the right primitive and the wrong thing to hand an artist: nobody wants to paint five faces to put down a clod of earth. The thing you place is a block, and a block is a RULE, not a fixed assignment — it says what each of its faces is made of given what is around it, and it is re-read whenever the neighbourhood changes.

Three lines. Everything else — which edge piece, which corner, where grass meets sand — is the material's own business, resolved from its archetype and the transitions.

Placed, and stacked
one block — grass lid, earth sides
another on top — the lid below is no longer open to the sky, so it is simply not drawn
three — and the sides are one continuous earth face, not three stacked ones

Nothing was reassigned by hand. The rule is evaluated per face against the neighbourhood, so stacking, digging and sculpting all keep the faces right for free.

Where it gets interesting
a step down

The block rule can say more than "top" and "side". A side face knows how far below the top it is, so a block can put a fringe of grass on the first band of its earth wall and plain earth below.

That is today's side field on a material, expressed as a rule rather than a second reference — and it stops being a property of the material at all.

Open, and worth arguing about
?

Is a block a BRUSH (it paints materials onto faces once, and they stay) or a TYPE the voxel remembers (its faces are re-derived forever)? The second is Minecraft's model and survives sculpting; the first is simpler and lets an artist override one face and keep it.

?

Can a rule see the neighbour's block, not just whether a face is covered? "Earth beside stone" would need it.

?

Does a block own its materials, or reference shared ones? Sharing means renaming Grass fixes every block; owning means a block is a self-contained thing you can hand to someone.
