Corrected again · whose vocabulary is it

The sixteen-tile corner set belongs to the floor archetype, not to materials

I had the corner set as universal, with seams bolted on for walls. That is backwards. An archetype owns a vocabulary of slots, and the floor archetype's vocabulary happens to be the sixteen-tile corner set. A wall's vocabulary is its own, and its seams are simply part of it rather than an exception to something else.

What follows from this
1

There is no universal tag. A slot is named by its archetype. The floor archetype's slot names happen to be corner masks; the wall's are words. Both are just slots.

2

Transitions are a floor idea, mostly. Two materials meeting at a corner is a corner question, so a transition block belongs to the floor archetype. Two wall materials meeting along a run is a different and much smaller question — a vertical seam, not sixteen tiles.

3

The block idea still holds. Whatever an archetype's slots are, they can be laid out and read from one point on a sheet. Floor reads a 5 × 3; wall reads a strip of seven; ramp reads four.

4

Mitring becomes an archetype's business. The wall archetype can say its convex and concave slots are optional and mitred when empty. The floor archetype has no such slot and never asks.
